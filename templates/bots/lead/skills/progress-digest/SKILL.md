# Progress digest

## Once a day. Once.

One summary per calendar day, on weekdays. If today's has already gone, stop
and say nothing. Two emails saying different things about the same day is worse
than neither, and a restart or a clock change must not produce a second.

Everything goes to the address the owner gave for summaries, which may differ
from the one on their applications.

## What goes in it, in this order

1. **Needs you** - at most three lines, at the top. Each is `Company - Role:
   what to do`, with a direct link. Rank by: a test or interview deadline
   first, then anything that unblocks a submit (a question only they can
   answer, a manual step, a recruiter waiting on them), then the rest. Within a
   tier, earliest deadline first; no deadline last. If more remain, one line
   saying how many and that they are in the attachment.
2. **One line of summary** - what went out since the last summary, plus the
   count of replies, tests and interviews.
3. **Replies** - tests and interview invitations: company, role, deadline,
   link.
4. **New and notable** - roles at companies the owner flagged as notify-only,
   up to ten lines of `Company - Role - City`, then a count for the rest.
5. **Numbers** - one line: total applied, waiting, stuck, skipped.

Skip any section that is empty. Attach the full list as a file; do not paste it
into the body. Aim for something readable in under thirty seconds.

## Dedupe

Repetition is what makes a daily email get ignored.

- A notify-only role appears once, ever. Record what you have already sent.
- An action item appears on the **first** summary after it shows up, then once
  a week as a reminder. Never the same parked item every morning.
- Test and interview deadlines are the reminder routine's job; the summary
  lists only **new** invitations.
- A posting that closed after the owner applied still counts in the total
  applied, and is not in the waiting, stuck or skipped numbers.

## Language

Plain words. The owner is not reading a dashboard.

- No file names, settings keys, model names, identifiers, hashes, error codes,
  or internal vocabulary. Company names and job titles stay as the employer
  writes them.
- If the owner has a preferred language set, write in it, and still keep
  company names and job titles in the original.
- Never explain how the system works inside. "Three applications need your
  answer" - not which part of the machine is waiting.
- **Never mention rejections** unless the owner has asked to see them. The
  record keeps every one; the summary does not repeat them back.

## If something is broken

If the mailbox could not be read, or the numbers could not be worked out, say
so in one line at the top of the summary rather than sending a confident
summary built on nothing. If a connection has failed twice across runs, pause
the routine and tell the owner in chat what to reconnect.

If literally nothing changed, send two lines on a weekday rather than silence:
a quiet day is information, and silence is indistinguishable from a broken
routine.
