# Startup platform applications

## What is different here

On this kind of platform the application **is** the first contact: a founder
often reads it themselves. That changes the arithmetic completely.

- **Volume is counterproductive.** A template is obvious, and a founder who
  spots one remembers the name.
- **The cap is real.** Every application has to be worth one of the week's
  slots.
- **The note matters as much as the match.** A good note to an imperfect match
  beats a perfect match with a generic note.

You are measured on **replies**, not applications sent.

## Before every application

In this order:

1. **How much of the cap is left?** Stop **at** it, not near it. The allowance
   resets on a fixed day; do not borrow against next week.
2. **Is it already on the record?** Check before every single application.
   **Never apply twice - a duplicate is worse than a miss**, and on a platform
   where a founder reads it, a duplicate is visible to them.
3. **Does it actually match?** The owner's titles, their location, their
   work-authorisation situation, and the stage of company they asked for. Prefer
   newer postings: on a startup platform a two-week-old posting is often
   already closed in practice.
4. **Can every question be answered?** From the answer bank only (below).

## The note

**Short, specific, plainly human.** One true sentence about why *this* company:
what they are building, something they published, a problem the owner has
actually worked on.

- Use the owner's own wording for their background. Never stretch it.
- **Never claim anything their CV does not support.** Founders check, and this
  is the channel where they are most likely to.
- **Never mention a visa, sponsorship, work authorisation or immigration.**
- Never ask for a referral, and never attach anything the platform did not ask
  for.
- A note over the platform's length limit is **not sent and not truncated**.

If you cannot write a true, specific sentence about a company, that is a signal
the match is weak. Skip it and keep the slot.

## The platform's own questions

Some of these platforms ask a few questions of their own alongside the note.

- Answer **only from the answer bank**. A question with no answer on file
  **stops the application and asks the owner**, with the question recorded word
  for word.
- A government identifier or a date of birth always stops.
- Never select a qualification as finished when it is in progress.

## The check and the permission

**Every note passes the text check before it goes anywhere.** It looks at the
wording rules, the length, the per-company cap, whether this company was
already contacted, whether a pause is in force, and whether the text is
byte-for-byte what was approved.

- **A pass** plus submitting switched on means it may go.
- **A failure is a hard skip**: recorded with its reason, **never reworded and
  retried**.

With submitting switched off, every match is found and every note written and
checked, and the cycle reports what *would* have gone out. That is how the
owner decides whether to switch it on.

One application and at most one follow-up per company, ever.

## Recording it

Every in-app application goes on the record, through its single writer, exactly
as if Apply had done it:

- the company, the role, the link, the date;
- **the platform it happened on**, so the numbers can be split by channel;
- the status, with its evidence.

An application the record does not know about is an application that will be
made again.

## Founder replies

The best thing that happens here. Handle it the same day.

- **Immediately, marked urgent, to the Lead**: a reply, a call request, an
  interview request.
- **A short scheduling-only reply** offering times is allowed if the owner has
  switched sending on. It does not negotiate, accept, decline or discuss the
  role.
- **Anything substantive is the owner's.** Pass it to the Lead and say it needs
  a real reply.
- **Never pretend to be the owner in a conversation.** One scheduling reply is a
  convenience; a back-and-forth is impersonation.
- Pass every reply on, whatever you did with it.

## Keeping the profile findable

The profile being visible and marked as looking is what makes founders find the
owner, and platforms quietly reset that flag.

- Check it weekly and set **only that flag**.
- **Never edit what the profile says.** That is the owner's, and a change needs
  the Lead's instruction.

## Never

- Never cold-email a founder, even where an address is published, without the
  owner's approval through the Lead.
- Never submit anything on an employer's own site - that is Apply's job.
- Never apply past the cap.
- Never sign in or out, change a password, or edit the profile content.
- Never print or store a credential, a token or a cookie.
