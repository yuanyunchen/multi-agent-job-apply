# Form answers

## The rule

> **An answer comes from the answer bank, or it does not exist.**

There is no inference, no sensible default, no "they probably meant yes". A
question you cannot answer truthfully from what is on file **parks the whole
application** and records the question word for word, so the owner can answer
it once and never again.

This is not caution for its own sake. An untruthful answer on an application is
the owner's problem, not the system's, and they will find out about it in an
interview.

## Always stop, whatever is on file

These park every time, however the answer bank is later edited:

- a national insurance or social security number;
- a date of birth;
- a driving licence or passport number;
- anything else that identifies the person rather than describing them.

If an answer bank somehow contains one, ignore it.

## What the bank holds

A handful of questions cover most forms. The bank matches on **wording that
may appear in the question**, not on an exact string:

| what it is for | typical wording |
|---|---|
| may you work in this country | "authorised to work", "legally authorized", "right to work" |
| will you need sponsorship | "sponsorship", "require sponsorship", "visa support" |
| would you relocate | "willing to relocate", "open to relocation" |
| earliest start date | "earliest start", "start date", "available to start" |
| salary expectation | "salary expectation", "compensation expectation", "desired salary" |
| how you heard about the job | "how did you hear", "how did you find" |

An entry with no answer is **not** an entry. A blank parks the question, which
is the correct behaviour and worth saying out loud to the owner.

## Short written answers

Some forms want a paragraph: why this role, why this employer, a relevant
project.

- Compose it **only from recorded background facts** - the notes the owner
  wrote for exactly this purpose.
- **Never go beyond them.** Not an inference, not a flattering rounding, not a
  claim the CV does not support.
- If the facts do not cover the question, **park it**. A paragraph you invented
  is a paragraph the owner has to defend.
- Keep it short, specific and plain. No template that would read identically to
  fifty employers.

## Choices and dropdowns

- **A date with no exact option**: pick the nearest option *after* the owner's
  date, then the earliest available. Read the selection back.
- **A qualification in progress is never selected as finished.** If the only
  options are finished or not started, park it.
- **A list of sources for "how did you hear about us"**: the real source is
  recorded on the row. Use it. Inventing a plausible one here is the single
  most tempting untruth on a form, and it has happened.
- **Anything with an "other, please specify"**: if the owner's answer is not in
  the list, use "other" and the answer bank's wording, not the closest thing in
  the list.

## Read it back

Before submitting, compare the filled form with what you meant to put in it:
name, contact details, location, work-authorisation answers, and the document
attached. A mismatch is a **block**, not a retry - something is overwriting
your input, and submitting anyway sends whatever that something decided.

Pay particular attention to any field the owner marked as never to be used -
an old address, a second email. Those are on the never-use list for a reason,
and an autofill helper does not know that.

## Attachments

Attach **exactly the file the owner named** - not a version of it, not a copy
you reformatted, not whatever a helper suggests. If the file is not where they
said it was, park the application and say so: an application with the wrong
document attached is worse than one that waited a day.

## Reporting

Every parked application is reported with **the exact question that parked
it**. Over a week those questions are the most valuable thing you produce: each
one, answered once, is an application that finishes by itself from then on.
